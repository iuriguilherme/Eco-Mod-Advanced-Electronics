# Advanced Electronics Strategy

## Target problem

Reaching advanced electronics in Eco is a large tech and labor investment that pays back
thin, uncompelling rewards — the late game goes hollow. Players who've climbed the tree
want a capability that makes the climb feel worth it.

## Our approach

Win by autonomy over gadgetry: ship a genuinely autonomous worker — the survey drone
roams, prospects underground, and reports on its own with no babysitting — so the payoff is
"a machine that works for you," not another handheld tool with a longer reach.

## Who it's for

**Primary (adopter):** Modded-server operators / curators — admins who add content mods to
keep a server fresh. They're hiring Advanced Electronics to give late-game players a
compelling new capability that extends the server's lifespan.

**Secondary (end user):** Late-game players running a settlement's mining / electronics
infrastructure — the ones who actually build, dispatch, and read the drone. The curator is
buying the mod *for* their experience.

## Key metrics

- **Active server installs (with retention)** — count of servers running the mod and whether
  they keep it across seasons / wipes, not cumulative downloads. Measured: mod portal +
  community reports. Lagging.
- **Drones built & deployed** — on servers that have it, do players actually craft and
  dispatch drones (does the payoff pull them in)? Measured: community reports, or in-mod
  counters if added. Leading.
- **Curator sentiment / re-recommend** — do admins rate it well and recommend it to other
  server operators? Measured: portal ratings, forums, Discord. Lagging.
- **SLG concept validation** — does Strange Loop Games engage with the concept as a candidate
  for official Eco? The north-star external signal; aspirational and rare, but falsifiable.
  Measured: SLG / community channels. Lagging.

## Tracks

### Autonomy engine

Navigation, pathfinding, sensing, and lifecycle — the drone roaming and prospecting unattended.

_Why it serves the approach:_ this IS the "machine that works for you"; without it there's no
payoff, only a gadget.

### The visible reward

Drawing survey areas on the map, assigning them, per-area persistent findings, and the dock
readout surfaces.

_Why it serves the approach:_ turns the drone's invisible work into a dig plan players can see
and act on — the payoff made legible.

### Progression depth

Recipes, tech-tier gating, and higher-tier drones (deeper sensors, larger areas).

_Why it serves the approach:_ makes the drone a real tech-tree payoff with room to grow,
directly answering the hollow-late-game problem.
